Muffins

Twelve tall, tender muffins with a proper crown — yogurt for the crumb, baking powder doing all the lifting on its own, and as much or as little sugar as you like.

Prep10 min
Bake18–20 min
Oven400°F / 200°C
Makes12

Method

The batter takes about four minutes. Everything else is getting ready for it.

  1. Heat the oven to 400°F / 200°C and line a 12-cup muffin pan with paper liners.

    Why firstThis batter goes into a hot oven the moment it's mixed. While the oven heats, take the milk, yogurt and eggs out of the fridge so they lose their chill.

  2. Sift 600 ml cake flour, 240 ml sugar, 3½ tsp baking powder and ½ tsp salt into a large bowl.

    If everything looks lump-free already, a good whisk does the same job.

  3. In a jug, whisk 120 ml milk, 120 ml yogurt, 160 ml oil, 2 eggs and 15 ml vanilla extract until smooth.

    Yogurt tipIf yours is thick Greek-style, whisk it into the milk on its own first so it loosens, then add everything else.

  4. Pour the wet into the dry and fold gently with a spatula, scraping right to the bottom of the bowl, until no dry flour is visible.

    Stop hereA lumpy batter is a good batter. Keep mixing past this point and the muffins turn dense and tough — this is the one step that decides how they come out.

  5. Divide between the 12 liners and get them into the oven straight away.

    Fill nearly to the top for proper domed crowns. An ice-cream scoop makes this quick and even.

  6. Bake on the middle rack for 18–20 minutes, checking from 18.

    Without the baking soda they'll be paler than usual at this stage. That's expected — go by the tests below instead.

  7. Rest a few minutes in the pan, then lift each muffin onto a wire rack.

    Left in the hot tin, they keep cooking and the bottoms go dry.

Are they done?

Three tests. You want all three.

RisenDomed and set, no dip in the middle.
FirmPress a top lightly — it springs back rather than wobbling.
Clean skewerIn and out of the centre with no wet batter clinging to it.

Good to know

Keeping them
Best the day they're baked, ideally still a little warm. The yogurt keeps them moist for a second and third day in a sealed container.
Freezing
Wrap each muffin once it's cool, seal them in a container, and freeze for up to three months. Thaw on the counter, or a few seconds in the microwave to bring back the just-baked smell.
Things to stir in
A handful of mini chocolate chips, frozen berries, chopped nuts, a grating of lemon or orange zest, or a little cinnamon. Fold them in at the very end of step 4 so you're not overworking the batter.
Finishing
A dusting of icing sugar once they're cool, or a thin vanilla glaze if you've got a few extra minutes.
No cake flour?
Plain flour works and still tastes excellent — the crumb is just a touch less delicate.
Cutting the sugar
Sugar does more than sweeten — it holds water in the crumb and browns the tops. At 75% you'll barely notice the difference. At 50% the muffins are noticeably less sweet, a little firmer, and paler, so check them a minute or two early and lean on the skewer test. A glaze or a dusting of icing sugar puts the sweetness back where you can taste it most, on top.
Is your baking powder still alive?
It's doing all the lifting here, so it's worth checking. Half a teaspoon in a glass of hot water should fizz straight away.
