Cocoa Browniesdense, fudgy, crackly on top

No chocolate bars to melt. Just a lot of cocoa, a lot of butter, and a bake that stops halfway for a rest. That pause is what gives you the flaky crust.

  • 16brownies
  • 20 minhands-on

What you need

  • Granulated sugar
  • Light brown sugar
  • Natural cocoa powderNot Dutch-process. Hershey’s or Ghirardelli unsweetened both work.
  • All-purpose flour
  • Kosher salt
  • Large eggs
  • Unsalted butter, melted2 sticks. Don’t cut this one.
  • Vanilla extract

Gear: an 8-inch square pan, parchment paper, cooking spray, a stand or hand mixer, and an instant-read thermometer if you have one.

Gram weights are close approximations. Cocoa is the one worth weighing, since it packs very differently depending on how you scoop.

How to make them

  1. Heat the oven to 300°F (150°C). Spray the pan, then lay in a strip of parchment that hangs over two sides by about 2 inches.

    WhyThose flaps are handles. You’ll lift the whole slab out in one piece later.

  2. Sift the granulated sugar, brown sugar, cocoa, flour and salt together into a bowl.

    WhyCocoa loves to clump. Sifting now means no dry pockets later.

  3. Beat the 4 eggs on medium for 2–3 minutes, until pale yellow and fluffy.

    WhyThe air you whip in here is what lifts the top into that thin, crackly layer.

  4. Drop to low speed. Spoon in the dry mix a little at a time, then pour in the melted butter and vanilla. Mix only until smooth, then scrape into the pan and spread it out.

    Heads upIt will be much thicker than boxed brownie batter. That’s correct, keep going.

  5. Bake for 15 minutes. Take the pan out and let it sit on the counter for 15 minutes.

    WhyThis pause is the trick. The top sets and dries while the middle stays soft, which is how you get a flaky crust over a fudgy center.

  6. Back into the oven for about 30 minutes, until the center reads 195°F (90°C).

    No thermometer?A toothpick in the middle should come out with moist crumbs, not wet batter.

  7. Cool in the pan for 30 minutes, lift out by the parchment, then give it 10 minutes on a rack. Cut into 16 with a long knife or a pizza wheel.

    BonusChill for an hour before cutting and they slice cleaner and get even fudgier.

What each ingredient does

Want to nudge the texture? This is what happens when you change one amount. Small moves of 10–20% are the safe way to experiment.

Make them even better

1–2 tsp espresso powderSift it in with the dry mix. Deeper chocolate, no coffee taste.
A pinch of flaky salt on topSprinkle before baking. Pairs best with the espresso.
About 1 cup chopped dark chocolateFold in last for melty pockets.
Brown the butterSame 2 sticks, cooked until golden and nutty, then cooled a little. Adds a toasty edge.
¾–1 cup toasted walnuts or pecansFor crunch against all that fudge.

Good to know

Does the cocoa type really matter?
Yes. Look for “natural” on the label and skip anything that says Dutch-process, alkalized or “processed with alkali.” Hot chocolate mix won’t work either.
Is there milk in this?
No. The eggs and melted butter are all the liquid it needs.
How much cocoa should I buy?
About 170 g. One 8 oz bag covers it. A 6 oz bag is cutting it close.
Storing leftovers
Stack them in an airtight container with parchment between layers. They keep for a few days at room temperature, assuming any are left.
